How to make a logo background transparent

By BonBon Labs · Updated October 14, 2026

A logo with a white box around it looks wrong on colored banners, slides and photos. Here is how to turn a logo picture into a transparent PNG for free, and when you need the original file instead.

What a transparent logo is for

A logo saved with a white box around it looks wrong on a colored banner, a slide or a photo. A logo saved as a transparent PNG has no box, so it sits cleanly on any color. It is the format most website builders, presentation programs and design tools want for logos.

Be realistic: This works best when your logo is a flat picture on a plain background, such as a JPG or a screenshot with a white or single-color background. If you still have the original from your designer, such as an SVG, AI or PDF file, use that for print and large sizes, since it is sharp at any size. Think of this method as a quick fix when all you have is a picture.

Step by step

  1. Find the biggest, cleanest version.A larger, sharper file gives cleaner edges. A tiny, blurry logo stays tiny and blurry.
  2. Open imgLop and choose your logo.On the home page, click Choose a photo and pick the file. PNG, JPG, WEBP and HEIC work, up to 15 MB. Each photo uses 1 of your 5 free daily credits, and no account is needed.
  3. Wait a few seconds.The AI runs in your browser, so your logo is not uploaded. The first run downloads the model once.
  4. Check the edges.Use the Slider view to compare the result with the original. Zoom in on thin lines, small text and curves. Try different backdrop swatches, such as a dark one, to reveal any leftover specks.
  5. Download the transparent PNG.Click Download PNG. This file stays transparent. The Download JPG with backdrop button makes a JPG instead, which cannot be transparent.
  6. Test it.Place the PNG on a light and a dark background in your slide or website and check that it looks right on both.

What works and what does not

Your logoResult
Solid shapes on a plain backgroundUsually clean.
Logo photographed on a shirt, sign or mugPossible, but check the edges closely.
Very thin lines, tiny text or soft shadowsMay lose detail or keep a faint edge.
White logo on a white backgroundHard for any tool. Find a version on a darker background first.

Tips

  • Keep logos as PNG (or WEBP). WEBP appears as a choice only if your browser supports it, so PNG is the safe pick. Saving a logo as JPG, in any imgLop tool, fills see-through areas with a solid color and the transparency is lost.
  • Changing a JPG to a PNG does not make it transparent. The Convert format tool keeps the transparency a PNG already has, but it cannot create transparency from a solid background. Remove the background first.
  • Start with the largest file. You can make a transparent PNG smaller afterward with the Resize & compress tab, by choosing PNG as the format (or WEBP, if your browser offers it), but you cannot add detail that was never there.
  • Several logos? Each one uses a credit, and the batch ZIP download is part of imgLop Pro, which is not available yet. Download each transparent PNG from its card.
  • Making a QR code? The QR maker has its own option for a transparent background, so you do not need this tool for that. See the QR printing guide.

Privacy: The background is removed in your browser. Your logo, including unreleased designs, is not sent to a server.

Questions people ask

Will the logo stay transparent when I upload it?

A PNG keeps its transparency. Some sites convert uploads to JPG, which fills transparent areas with white. If that happens, look for a setting that keeps PNG, or ask the site's support.

Is it free?

Yes. You get 5 free AI background removals per day with no account.

Is my logo uploaded?

No. The background removal runs in your browser on your own device.

Can I get a vector (SVG) logo from this?

No. The result is a PNG picture. A vector file has to come from the original design file, or from a designer.

The edges look rough. What can I do?

Start from a larger, sharper file, and one with strong contrast between the logo and its background. Fine lines, tiny text and soft shadows are the hardest cases.
